Q1. What is differential curvature?

Differential curvature occurs when the objective function has different curvature in different directions, causing gradients to have very different magnitudes and slowing gradient descent.

Q2. Why is L=x
2
+y
2
 easier to optimize than L=x
2
+4y
2
?

Because the first function has equal curvature in all directions, so the gradient points directly toward the minimum. The second has unequal curvature, leading to oscillations and slower convergence.

Q3. Why is the gradient perpendicular to contour lines?

Along a contour line, the function value is constant, so there is no change in the direction of the contour. Therefore, the direction of maximum increase (the gradient) must be perpendicular to the contour.

Q4. Why does gradient descent zig-zag?

Because one direction has much larger gradients than the other. The optimizer repeatedly overshoots in the high-curvature direction while making only small progress in the low-curvature direction.

Frequently Asked Theory Questions
Q1. Define Optimization.

Finding parameter values that minimize the loss function.

Q2. What is Gradient Descent?

An iterative optimization algorithm that updates parameters in the direction opposite to the gradient to minimize the loss.

Q3. What is Overfitting?

Excellent training performance but poor test performance because the model memorizes the training data.

Q4. Why is Feature Scaling Needed?

To make feature magnitudes similar, reducing oscillation and improving convergence speed.

Q5. Difference between Local and Global Minimum?
Local Minimum	Global Minimum
Lowest in nearby region	Lowest in entire function
May not be optimal	Best possible solution
Q6. What is Differential Curvature?

Different directions of the loss surface have different curvature, causing uneven gradients and zig-zag motion.

Q7. Why is Gradient Perpendicular to Contour Lines?

Because moving along a contour does not change the function value, while the gradient points in the direction of maximum increase.

Q8. Why does Gradient Descent Zig-Zag?

Because one direction has much larger gradients than another, leading to repeated overshooting.

Q9. What are Flat Regions?

Areas where the gradient is close to zero, causing very slow updates.
